Verdict

Engage is a big, ambitious internal tool that already does the "browse products, price a cart, produce an order form" job well enough that Branden got a quote within AED 10k of the estimation team. The two things you actually want it for, walk-and-shoot site surveys that produce a PAS 13 risk assessment and a drawing tool the team will use on a tablet, are the two least finished parts of it.

The most important finding: there is no image analysis anywhere in the app. Photos taken during a survey are stored as base64 strings in Postgres and printed into the PDF, but no AI ever looks at them. The risk level on each area is a dropdown pre-filled from a hard-coded table keyed on area type. The "AI chat" accepts a photo upload with the prompt "Please analyze this image" and then strips the image before calling OpenAI. Your vision of "take pictures, identify problems, build a PAS 13 risk assessment" is not partially built. It is unbuilt, on top of a survey form that is a reasonable skeleton for it.

Three other things frame everything below:

  • The team's May 5 feedback was mostly actioned on May 6. Request Quote flow removed, customer-info step removed, Solution Finder hidden, category filters fixed, dropdown flicker fixed, per-metre prices on survey recommendations, the 2.5 m price-doubling logic replaced, an accessory picker added, a server-side order-form PDF v2 started. Several of those fixes are partial, and one went backwards: the new order-form PDF has no images at all, where the old one did. Nothing has shipped since 7 May.
  • Mobile "lag" has a concrete cause. The catalogue page fires one request per product family for base plates on load, 59 API calls and 2.7 s of network before the page settles, plus ten 401s from auth probes on every public page. Voice notes cannot work in production because the security headers disable the microphone. These are hours to fix, not weeks.
  • The codebase has grown faster than it has been pruned. A 6,300-line worker entry file where 5,900 lines are one-off migration endpoints served as live HTTP routes, 14 orphaned components, an orphaned 623-line Meeting Notes page, a 941-line matching engine never imported, 394 MB of uploaded assets committed to git, no typecheck in CI, and an end-to-end test that runs against production rather than the PR. None of this blocks users, but it makes every fix slower and riskier than it should be.

My recommendation is a short stabilisation pass (security, performance, the open feedback items), then rebuild the survey around a photo-first, AI-assessed flow as the flagship, then rebuild the drawing tool's interaction model for touch. Detail in the plan.

Site survey

How it works today

Capture
  1. Create survey: title, facility, logo, location, requested-by contact. Pre-filled from the active project. Autosaved to local storage for offline.
  2. Add "Area of Concern": zone, area name, area type (12 hard-coded types), condition, risk level, issue description with voice dictation, vehicle weight/speed/angle, Matterport link, up to 8 photos.
  3. Photos: rear camera or gallery, resized to 1280 px JPEG at 0.8, stored as base64 inside the area row's JSON column.
Assess and output
  1. Impact calc per area: KE = ½ m (v sin θ)², filter products with rating ≥ KE, pick one per family at ~25 % margin.
  2. PAS 13 recommender runs the shared verdict engine and shows top 3 aligned barriers per zone.
  3. Outputs: client-side jsPDF report (cover, summary, per-area pages with photos, product cards, roadmap, methodology), Build Project to cart, AI quote draft, Complete Survey.

Where it falls short of the vision

  • No photo intelligence. Nothing reads the images. Risk level is chosen by the rep from a dropdown. The "Risk Assessment" paragraph shown per area is canned copy per area type. SiteSurvey.tsx:105-178, 2014-2017
  • Two competing safety calculations. The survey's own impact calc uses a 0 % safety margin, raw Math.sin, and ignores load mass; the PAS 13 engine below it uses a 30 % minimum margin and the standard's tables. The same area can show a product as "recommended" in one box and "not aligned" in the next. siteSurveys.ts:275-287 vs pas13Rules.ts:52, 399-416
  • Racking override recommends under-rated product. A RackGuard is force-injected for racking areas even when its rating is below the calculated energy, with a prose justification. siteSurveys.ts:370-423
  • "Build Project" modal is broken. It reads product.id and product.name but the data has productId and productName, so every product in an area shares one quantity input and cart lines get an undefined name. SiteSurvey.tsx:2394-2481
  • Vehicle never reaches the recommender. Suggested vehicles are appended as text into the issue description; the code that reads area.suggestedVehicle for the PAS 13 lookup finds nothing, so it always falls back to a closest-analogue path. SiteSurvey.tsx:915, quote.ts:837
  • Costs shown per zone are 1-metre prices. The recommender's cost estimate defaults to 1 m and quantity 1 when no length is passed, and the survey never passes a length. barrierRecommender.ts:501-522
  • Phantom surveys on flaky signal. The offline hook auto-posts the draft when connectivity returns if a title has been typed, then the explicit Create button posts a second one. SiteSurvey.tsx:407-414
  • Photo payload. Eight photos become roughly 4 MB of base64 in one POST and one Postgres row. The camera component's own docstring says the page should re-upload to R2; it never does. No EXIF orientation handling on the fallback path; HEIC silently produces broken thumbnails and missing PDF photos.
  • Voice notes are dead in production. Permissions-Policy: microphone=() blocks the Web Speech API the button relies on. securityHeaders.ts:91
  • No input validation on any survey route. Request bodies are spread straight into updates. A client can change a survey's userId or status. Four TODOs say "add Zod validation". siteSurveys.ts:62, 101, 180, 213
  • Add-to-cart takes two taps because the first tap only fetches and sets state; the modal mounts on the next render.

What the survey should become

The observational survey report is, by the team's own account, the single highest-converting artefact they produce, and reps do them on phones. That argues for making the survey the flagship rather than one of five entry points. The flow I would build:

  1. Walk and shoot. Camera-first screen. Each photo is uploaded to R2 immediately (queued offline), tagged with GPS and the current zone. A one-tap "same zone / new zone" control between shots, optional voice note per photo.
  2. AI reads each photo. A vision model returns structured JSON: what is visible (racking, dock, pedestrian route, column, existing barrier and its condition, floor type, damage evidence), the likely vehicle class from context, a suggested area type, hazard tags, and a plain-English observation. The rep confirms or corrects in one screen rather than filling in ten fields.
  3. Deterministic PAS 13 assessment. Feed the confirmed observations into the engine you already have: vehicle class from the T1-T4 thresholds, energy with load mass and the standard's sine table, 30 % margin, aligned barrier families per zone. The AI proposes, the engine decides; that keeps the report defensible.
  4. Risk register. Each zone gets likelihood × severity from a small rubric (vehicle class, traffic density, pedestrian exposure, existing protection, observed damage), and a priority ranking across the site. This is the "risk assessment aligned with PAS 13" the report currently only gestures at.
  5. One proposal document. Generated server-side: cover, executive summary, risk map, per-zone page with the annotated photo, observation, PAS 13 verdict, recommended barrier with datasheet thumbnail and budget line, then the layout drawing if one exists, then the budgetary order form. This replaces both the survey PDF and the order-form PDF the team criticised.
  6. Before/after. Store the survey as a snapshot so a return visit six months later can be compared zone by zone. Tom raised exactly this on the call.

Model choice: the vision step is the only new AI dependency. Claude's vision is strong at this kind of structured scene description and the app already has an OpenAI key, so either works. I would put the call behind one service function with a fixed JSON schema so the model can be swapped later.

Layout drawing

How it works today

  • Upload PDF, image, or DWG/DXF (DWG only gets a download link, no renderer). Files are stored in R2 with a .bin extension because the extension override was never implemented. 100 MB cap. A blank 4000×4000 graph-paper canvas is also offered.
  • Rendering is hybrid: an <img> for images, react-pdf for PDFs with tiered rasterisation, and one SVG overlay on top for all markups.
  • Scale calibration: click two points on a known dimension, type the length in millimetres. No metres, feet, or 1:100 ratio entry.
  • One drawing tool: a polyline bound to a cart item. A single click makes a point marker. Corners are auto-detected and corner posts are auto-added to the cart as a side effect. No text, symbol, rectangle, dimension, or wall/obstacle tool.
  • Quantity per product is the number of markups, not total length, so a 60 m run and a 2 m run each count as one.
  • Overlay has two modes: a technical top view with post circles and rail rectangles at real physical width once calibrated (genuinely good), or a schematic stroke. Colour is per cart-item index from a 15-colour palette, not per product family, and the on-canvas numbering scheme differs from the A/B/C key in the title block.
  • Export is PDF only, via html2canvas screenshot of the title-block frame into an A3 jsPDF. A print-preview toggle exists.
  • Live PAS 13 guardrails: scale-not-set, post-centre spacing, deflection zone obstructed, vehicle class mismatch, anchor/floor mismatch, underfloor services. Error-level findings gate "Transfer to cart" behind a confirm.

Why it feels clunky

  • Mouse and touch are two separate code paths. Pointer handlers bail on pointerType === 'touch' and a parallel set of touch handlers reimplements drawing, pan, and pinch. Right-angle snapping only exists on the mouse path. Long-press menus promised in the help dialog do not exist. index.tsx:755, 876, 932; useCanvasDrawing.ts:339-585
  • Undo is destructive and wrong. Cmd+Z deletes the last markup from the server, no redo, and because the server returns newest-first while local state appends, after a refetch undo removes the oldest markup. index.tsx:1153-1162
  • Stale markups leak between drawings. Opening a drawing with no markups leaves the previous drawing's markups on screen. index.tsx:228-232
  • Repositioning corrupts the origin. Only the path and end point are sent; the start coordinates keep the old location, and calculated length is never recomputed. index.tsx:772-777
  • Auto-align rewrites every markup with no confirm and no undo. index.tsx:1037-1138
  • The main component is 2,308 lines with 34 useState hooks, the drawing hook adds 25 more and returns a fresh 40-key object every render, and the overlay takes about 70 props with no memoisation. Every pan or zoom re-renders every markup. That is the lag on a tablet.
  • Product width and product id are never persisted on a markup; they are re-derived from the product name by regex on every load.
  • The deflection-zone rule almost never fires because there is no way to draw a wall; obstacles are inferred from product-name regex.

What it should become

The product idea is right: a marked-up plan with A-SAFE products as a legible overlay, usable on a tablet on site. The interaction model needs rebuilding rather than patching:

  • One pointer-events code path for mouse, pen, and touch, with pinch-zoom and two-finger pan as gestures on the container, not on the drawing tool. Snapping and long-press work identically everywhere.
  • A small, explicit tool set: select, barrier run (polyline), bollard/column guard (stamp), wall/obstacle, dimension, text note, and calibrate. Symbols come from a product-family library so the overlay is colour-and-shape coded per family, matches the title-block key, and the legend is generated from what is on the canvas.
  • Markups as a single versioned document (one JSON per drawing with an undo/redo history) instead of one row per stroke and a server round-trip per undo. Save on debounce, keep the last N versions.
  • Quantities from geometry: total length per family, post count from the run length and post-centre spacing, corners as a derived count the rep can override. That feeds the cart directly and removes the "one markup = one unit" surprise.
  • Vector PDF export of the drawing with the overlay and title block, rather than a screenshot, so it prints sharp at A3 and can be embedded in the proposal document.
  • Keep the PAS 13 guardrails; give them a real wall tool to check against.

Pricing, cart, order form

How a price is built today

  • Source. The 2025 v1 AED price list (PRL-1019, November 2025) is loaded by a script into per-SKU variant prices. Each product also gets a headline price (the shortest SKU) and a basePricePerMeter (the per-metre rate of the longest, cheapest SKU). A separate product_pricing table holds four quantity tiers as text columns. There is no price-list table as such.
  • Currency. Everything is AED internally. Conversion happens only in the browser, using live rates fetched from a free exchange-rate API on every request with hard-coded fallbacks. The worker never converts. Two different fallback rate sets exist, one on the server and one on the client. A currency_rates table exists and is never used.
  • Discounts. Reciprocal value commitments, partner codes (capped 15 %), LinkedIn social, a Standard/Silver/Gold/Platinum cap by subtotal (25 to 30 %), combined ceiling 40 %. All of that lives in a shared file the server never imports.
  • Delivery and installation are percentage uplifts (delivery 9.63 %; install 11.5 / 19.4 / 26.3 % for simple / normal / complex). The constants are copy-pasted in six places, and a seventh copy in the Add-to-Cart modal uses a different number (23.57 %).
  • Accessories (floor type, fixings, dock buffers, steel plates, L-brackets, height extension) are descriptive only. They add nothing to the price. Base plates are a separate table, also not priced into the cart.
  • VAT exists only on the AI quote draft (5 %). The cart and order form are ex-VAT throughout. The v2 PDF prints a VAT line and then labels the band "Grand total (ex. VAT)".

What to do about pricing

The individual fixes are small; the structural fix is one pricing module. Put one function in shared/ that takes cart lines, complexity, currency, discounts, and service package, and returns every subtotal, and have the cart, order creation, order-form page, PDF, and quote draft all call it. Then the numbers agree by construction and Sagarika's 10-to-15-quotation check becomes a real test suite. Add server-side enforcement of the discount ceiling and a stored currency plus rate at order time. Price accessories, or label them clearly as "for the estimation team, not priced".

Also worth deciding: whether the budgetary tool should show VAT at all. Today it shows it in one place and not the others.

Platform, mobile, security

Navigation

  • The Projects page is not in the sidebar. It is the 2,499-line heart of the app and is only reachable from the project switcher's "Manage projects" or global search. This is the direct cause of Shadab's "I created a project and cannot find it".
  • Broken links: Cart → /order-form (route needs an id), Start New Project → /draft-projects (no such route), Solution Finder → /impact-calculator (route is /calculator), Industry Case Studies → GET /api/login (POST-only).
  • Solution Finder is hidden from the sidebar but still linked from the Dashboard card and Start New Project. Analytics, Layout Drawings, and Calculations History are reachable only from deep links.
  • The "Administration" group shows for every user; non-admins get bounced to an admin login.
  • Five entry points on "Start New Project" was Tom's own design, but the team consistently used one (Browse Products). Worth collapsing to: Survey (flagship), Drawing, Catalogue, with the calculator as a tool inside each rather than a starting point.

Security

The basics are good: bcrypt, KV sessions with httpOnly cookies, Turnstile, per-email lockout, per-IP rate limits, strict CSP and HSTS, per-user tenant isolation, bearer-gated migration endpoints. Three things need attention:

  • Bootstrap endpoints are permanent GETs with credentials in the URL. /api/bootstrap-admin?token=&password= resets the admin password to any value and echoes it back; /api/bootstrap-user?token=&email=&password=&role=admin creates any user at any role. Both are gated on one env secret, but query strings land in Cloudflare logs and browser history. One leaked token is full takeover. Remove both now that the admin exists, or convert to a one-shot CLI script. worker/index.ts:2451, 2546
  • Admin promotion happens as a side effect of reads. GET /api/admin/session promotes a user to admin if an admin_users row matches their email; the admin login does the same. Consolidate to one explicit promotion path.
  • Twelve routes accept unvalidated JSON bodies and spread them into updates (surveys, areas, most of safety.ts). Ownership fields are writable by the client.

Engineering health

  • worker/index.ts is 6,283 lines; roughly 5,900 are 67 one-off schema, seed, ingest, and fix endpoints served as live routes. Move applied migrations to a migrations folder and delete the routes.
  • No typecheck anywhere: tsc is never run in CI or build. The single Playwright test runs against production, so a PR check can never catch a regression in the PR, and it passes green with zero assertions when the secrets are absent.
  • Dead code worth deleting: MeetingNotes.tsx (623 lines, no route), 14 orphaned components (about 2,800 lines), safetyMatching.ts (941 lines, never imported), whatsapp.ts (no call sites), useKeyboardShortcuts, the forum and safety-metrics routes with no UI. About 6,000 lines of client and worker code with no path to a user.
  • 394 MB of attached_assets (1,012 files: photos, brand guide, CSV price lists) is committed to git. Move to R2 and purge from history.
  • Apple Sign-In is fully built server-side but the button is commented out pending credentials. Account deletion has a careful GDPR endpoint and no button.
  • Both Zod schemas for surveys exist in shared/schema.ts and are unused. Wiring them in is a small task with a big safety payoff.

Recommended plan

Ordered by risk removed per day of effort. Phases 1 and 2 are what lets you roll out to the team with confidence; 3 and 4 are the product you described. Effort is my estimate for me working with sub-agents, not a promise.
